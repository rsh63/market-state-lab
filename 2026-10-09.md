## 4. Initial claims fall while continuing claims edge higher

![Abstract U.S. labor-market claims, economic monitoring, and monetary-policy illustration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab category illustration; not an event photograph.*

**Fact:** The Labor Department’s October 8 release put initial claims at 197,000 for the week ended October 3, down 2,000 from the revised prior week. Continuing claims rose 17,000 to 1.716 million for the week ended September 26. [U.S. Department of Labor, October 8](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf).

**Inference:** Low layoffs and difficulty finding a new job can coexist; these series measure different labor-market flows.

**Why it matters:** Use first-release vintages and model initial claims, continuing claims and hiring separately.

### Model hygiene

Control leakage, survivorship and look-ahead bias, first-known timestamps, corporate actions and stale options quotes. Keep research and validation data separate; retain failed trials, calibrate out of sample and monitor feature, probability, policy, cost and regime drift.

### MATLAB optimization

- Align asynchronous feeds with `timetable`, `retime` and `synchronize`; preserve original timestamps and avoid filling future observations backward.
- Vectorize rolling features, preallocate episode buffers and profile environment steps before parallelizing.
- Run parallel `bayesopt` across independent purged folds with reproducible random streams; keep the final holdout untouched.
- Use GPUs for sufficiently large neural-network batches; separate market-data, environment, execution-cost, constraint and agent modules.
