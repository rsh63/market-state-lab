## 4. Strong August payrolls reset the near-term Fed distribution

![Abstract U.S. employment, Federal Reserve, and interest-rate graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve and economics graphic.*

**Fact:** U.S. nonfarm payrolls increased by 162,000 in August, above the Reuters consensus of 56,000, while unemployment held at 4.1%. Labor-force participation rose to 61.6%, the average workweek was 34.4 hours, and average hourly earnings increased 3.1% from a year earlier. Rate-futures pricing put the probability of a September increase near 62% after the report. [Reuters payrolls report, September 4](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-nonfarm-payrolls-surge-august-unemployment-rate-steady-41-2026-09-04/), [Reuters Fed analysis](https://www.reuters.com/business/fed-rate-hike-back-focus-after-strong-jobs-report-2026-09-04/)

**Inference:** The report reduced immediate labor-downside concern and increased sensitivity to the next inflation data, especially at the front end of the Treasury curve.

**Uncertainty:** More than 60% of the payroll gain came from leisure and hospitality plus local-government education, revisions remain possible, and market probabilities are not FOMC commitments.

**Why it matters:** Preserve hold and hike regimes rather than averaging them. Model payroll breadth, wage growth, inflation, expected policy rates, term premium, and Treasury supply separately.
