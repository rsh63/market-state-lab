## 1. Data-retention concerns curb enterprise use of frontier AI

![Abstract enterprise-AI, data-security, and model-governance graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-1-ai.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab AI graphic.*

**Fact:** Palantir, Nvidia and Booz Allen Hamilton have restricted or reconsidered use of advanced AI models over proprietary-data and intellectual-property concerns, according to The Information as reported by Reuters. Palantir sought an irrevocable zero-data-retention commitment from Anthropic, Nvidia limited Anthropic models to less-sensitive work, and Booz Allen barred their use for proprietary cyber tasks. [Reuters, September 14](https://www.reuters.com/business/palantir-nvidia-curb-ai-model-use-over-data-fears-information-reports-2026-09-14/)

**Inference:** For sensitive agent deployments, retention policy, deployment boundary and auditability are becoming as important as benchmark performance.

**Uncertainty:** Reuters cited The Information and did not independently verify the restrictions. Company-wide scope, duration, exceptions and vendor responses remain unclear.

**Why it matters:** Redact confidential inputs, test data egress, require explicit retention guarantees, isolate credentials and maintain provider-specific audit logs for MATLAB-connected agents.

## 2. Private credit’s technology exposure has become a quant risk factor

![Abstract private credit, technology lending, and quantitative-finance graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-2-quant.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab quantitative-finance graphic.*

**Fact:** BIS research published September 14 found that U.S. technology borrowing through direct lending rose from about $22 billion in 2010 to more than $1 trillion in 2025, reaching nearly 45% of private-credit exposure. The study attributes much of the post-2020 expansion to demand from software and AI firms that borrow against recurring revenue and intangible assets. [Bank for International Settlements](https://www.bis.org/publications/qr-202609/financing-digital-economy-role-private-credit)

**Inference:** AI and software financing increasingly links public-equity concentration to opaque private marks, refinancing conditions and non-bank lender exposure.

**Uncertainty:** The analysis uses PitchBook direct-loan data and does not establish causality. Coverage, private valuations and covenant terms are incomplete; the BIS says sustainability and financial-stability effects remain open questions.

**Why it matters:** Add private-credit proxies, BDC performance, credit spreads, capex revisions and refinancing calendars as slow state variables. Do not treat private valuations as executable market marks.

## 4. High mortgage rates keep a U.S. housing rebound elusive

![Abstract U.S. housing, mortgage rates, and Federal Reserve transmission graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve and economics graphic.*

**Fact:** A Reuters poll conducted August 28–September 14 forecast the average U.S. 30-year mortgage rate at 6.60% and 6.52% over the next two quarters, higher than June forecasts. The current average was about 6.85%, while Case-Shiller 20-city home-price growth was forecast at 1.5% in 2026 and 2.3% in 2027. [Reuters, September 15](https://www.reuters.com/business/high-mortgage-rates-keep-us-housing-market-revival-elusive-2026-09-15/)

**Inference:** Long yields, term premium and federal borrowing may keep housing finance restrictive even if the Fed’s short-rate path later eases.

**Uncertainty:** These are poll forecasts, not outcomes. Mortgage spreads, Treasury yields, inventories, regional supply and policy implementation can change the path materially.

**Why it matters:** Model mortgage rates, transaction volumes, inventories, starts, home prices and credit performance separately. Avoid treating a policy-rate move as a one-for-one mortgage-rate signal.

## 5. Grab’s Atome deal pushes the super-app deeper into credit

![Abstract digital lending, buy-now-pay-later, and technology-business graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-5-business.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab technology/business graphic.*

**Fact:** Grab agreed to acquire 60% of Atome Financial for $1.49 billion and plans to buy the remaining 40% under a performance-based arrangement. Atome offers buy-now-pay-later products, consumer loans and digital lending; the transaction is expected to close in the third quarter of 2027. [Reuters, September 15](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/grab-takes-majority-stake-atome-financial-149-billion-deal-2026-09-15/)

**Inference:** Grab is shifting further from fee-based platform economics toward underwriting, funding and balance-sheet risk.

**Uncertainty:** Closing, regulatory approval, credit losses, funding costs, integration and the performance-based terms remain unresolved. Management’s 2028 targets are forward-looking.

**Why it matters:** Track originations, delinquencies, loss reserves, cost of funds, take rates, cross-sell, capital intensity and cash conversion together.
