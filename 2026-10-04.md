## 2. IEX Options fee framework puts total venue costs in focus

![Abstract options routing, exchange connectivity, and execution-cost illustration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-2-quant.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab category illustration; not an event photograph.*

**Fact:** A September 29 Federal Register notice made IEX Options non-transaction fees operative October 2. Permit and premium-product fees receive a full discount for the first three calendar months and a half discount for the next three; these are not transaction-fee holidays. [Federal Register, September 29; operative October 2](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-29/html/2026-19867.htm)

**Inference:** A new route must justify its total cost, including connectivity and data.

**Uncertainty:** Actual launch activity, executable depth, routability, and the latest transaction-fee schedule were not verified.

**Why it matters:** Compare fill probability, adverse selection, access and data costs, and implementation shortfall. Do not infer liquidity from a launch date or introductory discount.

### Execution implications

- Separate the Sunday futures reopen, Monday premarket, 8:30 a.m. CT cash auction, and first 30 minutes. Friday volume is an activity proxy, not a measure of Monday executable depth.
- Require fresh spreads, depth, quote age, and auction imbalances. Use limits, participation caps, cancellation controls, and realized-slippage kill switches.
- Rebuild SPY/QQQ volatility, skew, open interest, and strike liquidity after reopening. Expired Friday options cannot seed Monday’s surface; dealer gamma remains unverified.
- Compare routes using fills and adverse selection alongside fees. Amortize fixed access/data costs realistically; stress partial fills and nonlinear impact. Reject stale, crossed, zero-bid, one-sided, or thin quotes.

### DRL Improvements for the MATLAB System

1. Add session and venue labels, quote age, applicable fee schedules, yield changes, VX slope, and auction state; expose each feature only after its first-known timestamp.
2. Enforce drawdown, concentration, turnover, liquidity, and daily-loss constraints outside the reward; penalize downside risk, non-executable fills, and implementation shortfall within it.
3. Randomize weekend gaps, depth collapses, partial fills, latency, fee changes, venue outages, and nonlinear impact; include fixed access costs in evaluation.
4. Use purged, embargoed walk-forward folds, frozen baselines, multiple reproducible seeds, and untouched session/venue regimes. Reject policies that fail independent risk gates.

### Model hygiene

Control leakage, survivorship and look-ahead bias, macro vintages, corporate actions, stale options quotes, and multiple testing. Preserve delisted instruments, historical memberships, and fee effective dates. Calibrate out of sample, retain rejected trials, and monitor feature, probability, policy, transaction-cost, and regime drift.

### MATLAB optimization

- Align market, macro, session, venue, and auction data with `timetable`, `retime`, and `synchronize`; preserve first-known timestamps.
- Vectorize rolling features, preallocate buffers, and profile environment steps before parallelizing.
- Run parallel `bayesopt` across independent purged folds with reproducible random streams and multiple seeds.
- Reserve GPUs for sufficiently large neural-network batches; separate environment, execution-cost, constraint, and agent modules.
