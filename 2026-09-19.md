## 2. Corporate FX hedge ratios fall to their lowest in MillTech's series

![Abstract foreign-exchange hedging, rate divergence, and quantitative-finance graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-2-quant.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab quantitative-finance graphic.*

**Fact:** A MillTech survey of 285 senior finance decision-makers at U.S. and U.K. companies found the average FX hedge ratio fell from 57% to 46% in the second quarter, the lowest since the survey began in early 2024. Average hedge length declined from 6.62 months to 5.7 months. [Reuters, September 18](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-uk-companies-cut-fx-hedges-lowest-since-2024-survey-shows-2026-09-18/)

**Inference:** Companies are preserving tactical flexibility, but lower and shorter protection can transmit currency shocks more quickly into margins and guidance.

**Uncertainty:** The sample is limited and self-reported. Hedge ratios do not reveal option structures, currencies, entry levels, natural hedges, or the full distribution of exposures.

**Why it matters:** Treat corporate hedging as a slow risk-state variable. Model currency, maturity, hedge instrument, carry, basis, liquidity, and earnings sensitivity separately.

## 4. U.S. factory production falls after seven monthly gains

![Abstract U.S. manufacturing, industrial capacity, and Federal Reserve graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve and economics graphic.*

**Fact:** Federal Reserve data showed manufacturing output fell 0.3% in August after seven consecutive increases. Overall industrial production was unchanged, and capacity utilization held at 76.3%, 3.1 percentage points below its 1972–2025 average. [Federal Reserve, September 18](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm)

**Inference:** AI-related investment is supporting selected industries, but energy costs, higher rates, and softer durable-goods output are limiting the breadth of industrial momentum.

**Uncertainty:** August data are preliminary and subject to revision. One month does not establish a manufacturing downturn, and industry-level dispersion remains large.

**Why it matters:** Keep output, capacity, energy costs, orders, inventories, and rates as separate state variables. Avoid converting a single aggregate release into a directional equity signal.

## 5. Oracle-linked data-center debt trades under pressure

![Abstract AI data-center financing, credit risk, and technology-business graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-5-business.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab technology/business graphic.*

**Fact:** The Financial Times reported that about $18 billion of loans tied to an Oracle-leased New Mexico data center were quoted by syndicate banks at 89–91 cents on the dollar. The project has faced local opposition and a blocked natural-gas-pipeline request; Oracle's corporate rating is one notch above junk after a July S&P downgrade. [Reuters, September 18](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/oracles-18-billion-data-center-debt-under-pressure-ft-reports-2026-09-18/)

**Inference:** AI infrastructure is moving from an equity-growth narrative into project-finance, permitting, power, and bank-balance-sheet risk.

**Uncertainty:** Reuters cited the Financial Times and received no comment from Oracle or the named banks. Loan quotes are indicative, and the project's final financing, construction, and operating outcome remain unresolved.

**Why it matters:** Track debt distribution, credit spreads, permits, power and water access, construction milestones, tenant concentration, utilization, and cash conversion together.
