## 1. FSB identifies frontier-AI cyber risk as an immediate financial-stability concern

![Abstract frontier-AI, cybersecurity, and financial-resilience graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-1-ai.png)

**Fact:** Financial Stability Board Chair Andrew Bailey told G20 finance ministers and central-bank governors that frontier AI could materially change the speed, scale, and economics of cyber risk. The August 31 letter also warned that dependence on a small number of technology providers could weaken system-wide confidence. [FSB letter, August 31](https://www.fsb.org/2026/08/fsb-chairs-letter-to-g20-finance-ministers-and-central-bank-governors-august-2026/)

**Inference:** AI concentration risk is becoming both a cyber-resilience problem and a third-party model-infrastructure risk for financial institutions.

**Uncertainty:** The letter identifies plausible systemic channels; it does not estimate incident probabilities, losses, or institution-specific exposure.

**Why it matters:** Treat external models, cloud services, and agent tools as critical dependencies. In MATLAB workflows, use narrow permissions, signed inputs, output validation, complete logs, fail-closed controls, and tested recovery paths.

## 4. Barclays shifts from no more 2026 hikes to September and December increases

![Abstract Federal Reserve, interest-rate, and Treasury-market graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve graphic.*

**Fact:** Barclays now expects two additional quarter-point Federal Reserve increases in 2026—September and December—after Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks. The bank previously expected no further increases this year; interest-rate futures assigned a 60.4% probability to a September increase. [Reuters, August 31](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/barclays-sees-two-more-fed-rate-hikes-this-year-after-warsh-speech-2026-08-31/)

**Inference:** The policy distribution is moving toward renewed tightening, increasing the sensitivity of front-end yields and growth equities to incoming labor and inflation data.

**Uncertainty:** Barclays' view is a forecast, not an FOMC decision. Market probabilities can move rapidly, and one employment or inflation release could materially change the path.

**Why it matters:** Preserve distinct hold, one-hike, and multi-hike regimes. Keep expected policy rates, term premium, Treasury supply, energy inflation, and market-functioning interventions as separate model inputs.
