## 2. A new review finds no demonstrated cross-regime alpha from general AI trading systems

![Abstract AI trading evidence, portfolio construction, and market-microstructure graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-2-quant.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab quantitative-finance graphic.*

**Fact:** A review submitted September 4 examined published evidence on AI in equity and crypto markets through August 31. It found no public demonstration of a general AI architecture delivering persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha, and argued that credible evidence requires point-in-time data, joint portfolio-and-execution evaluation, controlled adaptation, and prospective testing. [Zhu and Hu, arXiv:2609.04917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04917)

**Inference:** The main bottleneck is not generating signals; it is proving that signals survive realistic costs, capacity limits, regime changes, and governance constraints.

**Uncertainty:** This is a review, not a new live trading experiment. Public evidence may omit proprietary successes, while publication and selection bias can distort both positive and negative conclusions.

**Why it matters:** Require point-in-time data, frozen benchmarks, realistic fills, capacity tests, and prospective paper trading before treating an AI strategy as investable.

## 4. Small-business optimism cools as sales and inflation concerns worsen

![Abstract U.S. small business, inflation, employment, and Federal Reserve graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve and economics graphic.*

**Fact:** The NFIB Small Business Optimism Index fell 1.1 points in August to 98.7, still above its 52-year average of 98.0. A seasonally adjusted net 9% of owners reported higher nominal sales, the weakest reading since November 2025, while inflation rose two points to 16% as the most important problem. [NFIB, September 8](https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/new-nfib-survey-main-street-optimism-cools-in-august-but-holds-above-long-term-average/)

**Inference:** Main Street is reporting a mixed nominal-growth environment: softer demand alongside persistent price pressure, which complicates the Fed’s inflation-growth tradeoff.

**Uncertainty:** The survey samples NFIB members rather than the full business population, responses are qualitative, and the index does not determine near-term monetary policy.

**Why it matters:** Keep sales, pricing, hiring, credit, and policy-expectation states separate. Use Thursday’s PPI and Friday’s CPI to confirm or reject the survey-based inflation signal.
