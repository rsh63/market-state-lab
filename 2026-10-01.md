## 4. PCE inflation and strong spending require separate signals

![Abstract consumer inflation, real spending, and Federal Reserve transmission illustration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab category illustration; not an event photograph.*

**Fact:** BEA’s September 30 release showed August headline PCE prices up 0.3% month over month and 3.4% year over year; core prices rose 0.2% and 3.0%. Real consumer spending increased 0.6%. The annual update revised monthly data back to January 2021. [BEA, September 30](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026)

**Inference:** Moderate monthly core inflation and firm demand can pull policy expectations in different directions.

**Uncertainty:** One month does not establish disinflation; revisions and subsequent data can change the interpretation.

**Why it matters:** Preserve first-known data vintages. Model inflation, real spending, income, and the yield response separately instead of backtesting on revised history alone.

### Model hygiene

Control leakage, survivorship and look-ahead bias, publication timestamps, corporate actions, stale options quotes, and multiple testing. Preserve first-known macro vintages and historical ETF membership. Calibrate out of sample, retain rejected trials, and monitor feature, probability, policy, transaction-cost, and regime drift.

### MATLAB optimization
