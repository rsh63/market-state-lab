## 2. Algorithmic trading / quantitative finance — Kalshi adopts Nasdaq market surveillance

*Image: Market State Lab original category graphic.*

**Facts.** Reuters reported on August 10 that Kalshi entered a multiyear agreement to deploy Nasdaq's surveillance technology in phases. The platform is intended to detect manipulation, abusive trading and potential insider activity in real time and support regulatory reporting.

**Inference.** Prediction markets are being pushed toward exchange-grade controls. Better surveillance may improve institutional confidence, but it can also expose strategies that rely on thin books, related-account activity or ambiguous information advantages.

### Execution risk: SPY, QQQ and options

- **Opening auction:** PPI lands one hour before the cash open. Premarket indicative prices may gap twice—on the release and again as the opening auction clears. Stage limits and wait for auction imbalance plus spread normalization.
- **Spreads, depth and slippage:** SPY/QQQ are structurally liquid, but top-of-book depth can thin abruptly in the first minutes. Benchmark implementation shortfall to arrival and auction prices; avoid treating quoted spread as total cost.
- **Options:** Post-data implied-volatility compression can hurt long premium even when direction is correct. For 0DTE contracts, gamma and charm make hedge ratios unstable; use executable bid/ask marks, minimum quote-age rules and fill-probability assumptions.
- **Liquidity traps:** Avoid far-OTM weekly strikes with attractive displayed midpoints but sparse prints, wide markets or one-sided size. Prefer strikes with continuous two-sided depth and verify the underlying/option timestamp alignment.

### DRL Improvements for the MATLAB System

1. **Constrained reward:** optimize risk-adjusted return while penalizing drawdown, turnover, inventory concentration and participation in thin books; add a terminal capital-preservation barrier.
2. **Regime-robust walk-forward:** train across rolling windows with volatility, liquidity and event-day labels; randomize regime sequences and reserve the newest regime for untouched evaluation.
3. **Transaction-cost realism:** simulate spread, nonlinear impact, partial fills, latency and adverse selection; make the agent choose both target position and execution urgency.
4. **Uncertainty gating:** use a distributional critic or ensemble dispersion to reduce exposure when policy disagreement or state novelty is high; stress the policy with block-bootstrap crisis episodes.

### Model hygiene

- Fit scalers, feature selectors and regime labels inside each training fold to prevent leakage and look-ahead bias.
- Use point-in-time universes to control survivorship bias; process splits, dividends, symbol changes and other corporate actions consistently.
- Reject stale or crossed option quotes; align option and underlying timestamps and retain bid/ask, not midpoint alone.
- Record every specification tried, adjust for multiple testing and keep a locked holdout. Recalibrate probabilities and monitor feature, residual, cost and policy drift.

### MATLAB optimization patterns

- Store market data as `timetable` objects and use `synchronize`/`retime` once; avoid repeated timestamp joins inside training loops.
- Vectorize rolling features and preallocate episode buffers; use `profile` before moving work to parallel or GPU execution.
- Run Bayesian hyperparameter search in parallel with reproducible `RandStream` substreams and purged walk-forward folds.
- Use GPUs for dense neural-network batches, not branch-heavy microstructure logic; keep the environment modular so cost, fill and risk models can be swapped independently.
