## 2. SEBI proposes revising expiry settlement after closing-auction volatility

![Abstract derivatives expiry, closing auction, and quantitative-finance graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-2-quant.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab quantitative-finance graphic.*

**Fact:** India’s securities regulator proposed two alternatives for derivatives-expiry settlement: combine the final 30 minutes of regular trading with the 10-minute closing auction, or use the final 30 minutes alone while separating derivatives from the auction for one year. Public feedback is due October 3. [Reuters, September 12](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-regulator-plans-changes-set-expiry-days-settlement-prices-derivatives-2026-09-12/)

**Inference:** Settlement design can change basis, hedging demand, volatility, queue behavior, and manipulation risk even when the underlying signal is unchanged.

**Uncertainty:** This is a consultation, not a final rule. The chosen method, implementation date, and realized liquidity effects remain unknown.

**Why it matters:** Maintain separate continuous-session, closing-auction, and expiry cost models. Preserve imbalance, cancel, queue, reference-price, and settlement metadata rather than pooling all end-of-day fills.

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve and economics graphic.*

One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.6% from 4.0%, while long-run expectations increased to 3.4% from 3.3%. [University of Michigan, September 11](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-consumer-sentiment-deteriorates-september-inflation-expectations-rise-2026-09-11/)
