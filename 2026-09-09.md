## 2. CME FX futures and options reach record open interest

![Abstract foreign-exchange futures, options, and quantitative-finance graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-2-quant.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab quantitative-finance graphic.*

**Fact:** CME reported that FX futures and options open interest reached a record 4,410,167 contracts on September 4, above the previous June record. The number of large open-interest holders reached 1,446 in the CFTC’s August 25 report, while asset-manager notional open interest exceeded $200 billion for the first time. [CME Group, September 7](https://www.cmegroup.com/media-room/press-releases/2026/9/07/cme_group_sets_newfxopeninterestrecord.html)

**Inference:** Currency hedging demand is broadening as rates, energy, and geopolitical shocks increase cross-border risk.

**Uncertainty:** Open interest is a stock of outstanding positions—not volume, directional exposure, executable depth, or proof of tight spreads. The figures are exchange-reported and aggregate heterogeneous contracts.

**Why it matters:** Do not substitute open interest for liquidity. Quant systems should evaluate each currency pair, maturity, session, spread, depth, roll, and realized fill quality separately.

## 4. Inflation expectations hold, but unemployment and household-finance concerns worsen

![Abstract U.S. household expectations, inflation, employment, and Federal Reserve graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-4-fed.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab Federal Reserve and economics graphic.*

**Fact:** The New York Fed’s August survey showed median one- and five-year inflation expectations unchanged at 3.6% and 3.0%, while the three-year measure slipped to 3.2%. The perceived probability that unemployment will be higher in one year rose to 44.4%, its highest since April 2020; credit-access and household-finance expectations also deteriorated. [Federal Reserve Bank of New York, September 8](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260908)

**Inference:** Consumers are describing a difficult mix of persistent price pressure and weaker perceived labor-market security, complicating the Fed’s inflation-growth tradeoff.

**Uncertainty:** The roughly 1,300-household internet panel was fielded through August 31, before the latest oil-price jump. Expectations are not realized inflation, employment, or FOMC decisions.

**Why it matters:** Keep inflation expectations, realized inflation, labor expectations, and market-implied policy probabilities separate. Require confirmation from PPI, CPI, and rates before changing regime labels.

## 5. Qualcomm’s Amazon agreement links AI-chip demand to equity warrants

![Abstract AI data-center chips, optical connectivity, and technology-business graphic](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rsh63/market-state-lab/main/story-5-business.png)

*Credit: original Market State Lab technology/business graphic.*

**Fact:** Qualcomm said Amazon could buy up to $60 billion of AI data-center chips and related products under a long-term agreement. Purchase-linked warrants could let Amazon acquire roughly $4 billion of Qualcomm stock at $161.26 per share. The companies will also develop optical-connectivity technology extending to 1.6 terabits per second. [Reuters, September 8](https://www.reuters.com/technology/qualcomm-amazon-develop-custom-chips-ai-data-centers-2026-09-08/)

**Inference:** AI-infrastructure competition is expanding from accelerators into inference silicon, optical interconnects, and customer-financing structures.

**Uncertainty:** The headline purchase amount is a ceiling, not recognized revenue. Vesting, order timing, margins, deployment performance, customer concentration, and dilution determine the economics.

**Why it matters:** Separate contracted capacity from revenue and cash flow. Track purchase milestones, warrant dilution, gross margin, optical attach rates, utilization, and delivery execution.

### Model hygiene

Control leakage, survivorship and look-ahead bias, first-known timestamps, corporate actions, stale options quotes, crossed markets, and multiple testing. Calibrate out of sample, retain rejected trials, and monitor feature, probability, policy, transaction-cost, and regime drift.
