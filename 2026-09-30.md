### Execution implications

- **7:30 a.m. CT:** PCE and GDP releases; **8:30 a.m.:** cash opening auction; **3:00 p.m.:** quarter-end cash close. Preserve pre-release data vintages through annual revisions. [BEA schedule](https://www.bea.gov/news/schedule)
- Refresh spreads, depth, quote age, and auction imbalances after the release. A displayed midpoint or high daily volume does not establish executable liquidity.
- Use auction-aware limits, participation caps, cancellation controls, and realized-slippage kill switches. Separate macro surprise from quarter-end flow effects.
- Rebuild SPY/QQQ volatility, skew, open interest, and strike liquidity. Dealer gamma direction is unverified; 0DTE hedging effects depend on actual positioning.
- Reject stale, crossed, zero-bid, one-sided, or thin options quotes. Do not extrapolate regular-session fill rates into extended or weekend trading.

4. Use purged, embargoed walk-forward evaluation with frozen baselines, multiple seeds, regime scorecards, and untouched macro-event and quarter-end blocks.

### Model hygiene

Control leakage, survivorship and look-ahead bias, publication timestamps, corporate actions, stale options quotes, and multiple testing. Retain first-known macro vintages through revisions. Calibrate out of sample and monitor feature, probability, policy, cost, and regime drift.

### MATLAB optimization

- Align market, macro, session, and auction data with `timetable`, `retime`, and `synchronize`; retain first-publication timestamps.
- Vectorize rolling features, preallocate buffers, and profile environment steps before parallelizing.
- Run parallel `bayesopt` across independent purged folds with reproducible random streams and multiple policy seeds.
- Reserve GPUs for sufficiently large neural-network batches; separate environment, cost, constraint, validation, and agent modules.
